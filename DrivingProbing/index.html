What Probing Reveals about Autonomous Driving:
Linking Internal Prediction Errors to Ego Planning

TL;DR

Strong closed-loop driving performance does not necessarily imply robust prediction and planning.

Overview. High closed-loop performance alone does not guarantee that a driving policy has learned robust prediction and planning capabilities. We therefore first establish Data Scaling Laws for BC and RL policies, showing that closed-loop performance improves as the training data scale increases. We then study this through four analyses—Linear Probing, Perturbed Simulation, Near-Collision, and Causal Intervention—to examine how models represent surrounding vehicles, respond to perturbations, anticipate safety-critical interactions, and adapt ego plans when predictions are modified.

Abstract

To better understand the limits and weaknesses of driving policies, we focus on probing for forms of prediction, i.e., where surrounding vehicles will move next, and planning, i.e., understanding how to generate safe trajectories. We focus on these two capabilities because they reflect behaviors expected of effective driving policies, and use their presence or absence to assess policy quality across data-driven behavior cloning and simulation-driven reinforcement learning policies. To evaluate the presence of these capabilities, we investigate them as a function of scale, asking whether the closed-loop gains from larger datasets and longer simulation training reflect stronger prediction and planning or merely better behavioral heuristics. We use linear probing and targeted perturbations in both imitation learning and reinforcement learning models to track when these internal signals emerge, plateau, or fail. Despite good closed-loop performance, policies often fail to form timely surrounding-vehicle predictions during near-collision events, revealing a limitation in the predictive signals available for ego planning. Finally, causal intervention shows that correcting mistaken predictions improves ego planning toward safer trajectories.

Data Scaling Laws

(a) Closed-loop scaling: We train BC and RL policies across increasing data scales and evaluate them with WOSAC metrics, including vehicle collision, off-road rate, and goal progress. (b) Performance is not explanation: Both policy families exhibit clear scaling trends: BC improves and begins to plateau near the larger-data regime, while RL reaches lower collision and off-road rates and high goal progress. These gains motivate the probing analyses below, which ask whether better closed-loop scores correspond to stronger internal prediction and planning.

Linear Probing

Top figure: We train linear probes on intermediate policy representations and compare them with raw-input probes. The F1 Macro and trajectory-type accuracy gaps show how much additional future-motion information is encoded inside the learned representation across data scales and prediction horizons. BC gains grow with scale and then plateau, RL keeps stronger probing gains across horizons, and SMART (IL) is especially stable for longer-horizon prediction, suggesting that different training paradigms preserve surrounding-vehicle information in different ways. Bottom figure: We then relate the probing signal to future ego-other distance. As scale increases, the representation becomes more sensitive to vehicles that will remain close to the ego, but it still does not perfectly isolate the truly safety-critical agents.

Perturbed Simulation

We randomly remove surrounding vehicles in closed-loop simulation to test whether policies robustly use interaction information or depend on the mere presence of other agents. If a model truly reasons about traffic, removing vehicles should make many scenes easier. Instead, performance can degrade in the single-AV setting, with worse off-road and progress trends, revealing a spurious reliance on surrounding vehicles that is only partially reduced by scale.

Near-Collision

We examine pre-collision windows with the surrounding-vehicle probe to ask whether policies identify the safety-critical vehicle early enough for avoidance. The heatmaps compare probing signal near collision against each model's average signal over the episode. Both BC and RL eventually emphasize the nearby colliding vehicle, but the strongest signal appears mainly in the final 10 steps before impact, indicating that relevant prediction often arrives too late to prevent the failure.

Causal Intervention

We intervene on internal surrounding-vehicle representations and observe how the decoded ego plan changes. In the adaptiveness setting, we perturb another vehicle's predicted future to create a potential conflict and test whether the ego plan avoids it. In the recovery setting, we replace an incorrect surrounding-vehicle prediction with the correct label and test whether the ego plan returns toward a safer, ground-truth-aligned trajectory. Across BC, RL, and IL, these interventions frequently change ego planning, showing that surrounding-vehicle predictions causally affect the planner.
