ReCord: Replay Coordination for Safe and Robust Zero-Shot Coordination in Autonomous Driving

What is reactivity reliance?

A policy can appear safe because another vehicle yields or avoids the conflict for it. Remove that favorable reaction, and collisions may reappear. ReCord trains the ego to adapt, rather than counting on its partners to adapt for it.

Same unseen partner, different execution. On map 7664, Reactive-PBT succeeds when the partner reacts but collides when that partner's actions are replayed. ReCord succeeds in both examples.

01

Why it matters

  1. Unseen maps are not unseen partners

    Self-play trains agents against copies of the same policy. Success on held-out scenes does not guarantee coordination with unfamiliar driving styles, including new RL policies or human drivers.

  2. In driving, either vehicle can avoid the crash

    A conflict can often be resolved if either the ego or the partner yields. Reactive-PBT exposes the ego to diverse partners, but those partners still act online. If they repeatedly yield first, the ego may learn to depend on that response instead of learning when to yield itself.

  3. Unseen drivers may not respond as expected

    Other vehicles may react differently, too late, or not at all. We therefore need to test not only whether the ego succeeds with an adaptive partner, but what happens when the same kind of partner no longer adapts to the ego.

02

How ReCord works

ReCord keeps the diversity of population-based training but changes how the partners are executed. Before ego training, it collects action traces from a population of pre-trained policies. During training, partner vehicles replay their stored actions instead of running their policies online. Their actions can no longer change in response to the ego's current behavior, so the ego must learn to handle conflicts without depending on favorable partner adaptation.

Reactive-PBT

Partner policy runs online

Partners observe the evolving scene and can yield, brake, or avoid the ego in response.

ReCord

Partner action trace replayed

Partners follow actions recorded in advance. The ego's current behavior does not alter their next action.

In the evaluated simulator, a replayed vehicle starts from the same initial state as its recorded counterpart. Deterministic vehicle dynamics reproduce its recorded kinematic trajectory, and non-terminating collisions preserve the full action trace. Importantly, ReCord does not use human log-replay trajectories as training partners; human log-replay is reserved as an unseen evaluation setting.

03

How we test reactivity reliance

We compare each ego policy against unseen partners under two execution modes: reactive partners select actions online from the evolving scene; replayed partners follow pre-recorded action traces. Matching the partner type and evaluation setup lets us examine how performance changes when online partner adaptation is absent.

US / USR

Unseen seeds. Same policy family but different random seeds. US is reactive; USR replays the unseen partners' actions.

UR / URR

Unseen rewards. Partners learned with different reward functions. UR is reactive; URR replays their actions.

LR

Human log-replay. Recorded human trajectories are non-reactive, and the human logs were not used in ReCord's training.

We describe reactivity reliance using the change in collision per agent between the matched replay and reactive evaluations (USR − US, or URR − UR). A smaller gap indicates less performance degradation when partner reactivity is removed, but it must be considered alongside collision and task-success performance in both modes.

04

What changes

The largest advantage appears when unseen partners do not react. ReCord is broadly competitive with Reactive-PBT under reactive evaluation, while achieving substantially fewer collisions with replayed partners and human logs.

Less dependent on partner reactions

With the L+N training population and uniform sampling, the matched replay-reactive collision gap shrinks for both unseen seeds and unseen rewards. The values below are collisions per agent, not percentage-point changes in collision rate; they are calculated from the four-seed results in the paper.

Unseen-seed reliance gap

0.123 → 0.045

USR − US, Reactive-PBT → ReCord. Replayed collisions fall from 0.213 to 0.147 per agent.

Unseen-reward reliance gap

0.126 → 0.053

URR − UR, Reactive-PBT → ReCord. Replayed collisions fall from 0.245 to 0.170 per agent.

Human log-replay collisions

0.451 → 0.293

Reactive-PBT → ReCord with prioritized sampling: 35.0% fewer collisions per agent.

The trade-off matters: under reactive unseen-seed evaluation with uniform sampling, Reactive-PBT has slightly fewer collisions (0.090 vs. 0.102 per agent) and a slightly higher success rate (97.5% vs. 97.0%). ReCord's advantage is therefore not that it wins every reactive test, but that its performance degrades less when the partner's adaptation disappears.

Better coordination with human drivers

Human log-replay evaluates ReCord on recorded trajectories that were not used as its training partners. With prioritized partner sampling, its collision count falls from 0.451 to 0.293 per agent, while success increases from 90.9% to 94.0% (+3.1 percentage points). It maintains an average speed of about 9.9 m/s, rather than avoiding conflicts by stopping. With uniform sampling, collisions also fall from 0.426 to 0.327 per agent and success rises from 90.7% to 93.0%.

These human-log benefits also hold across predefined reward-based and unsupervised ME populations, as well as uniform, curriculum, and prioritized sampling. On log-replay, structured sampling further reduces ReCord's collision count; the same improvement is not observed consistently for Reactive-PBT.

05

Nearly 3× faster training

ReCord replaces repeated partner-policy inference with lightweight action replay. Under the same training and hardware configuration, it processes roughly three times as many environment steps per second. This advantage holds across uniform, curriculum, and prioritized partner sampling.

126K vs 42K

Raw steps per second · uniform sampling · single RTX 3090

ReCord reaches roughly 126K raw SPS, compared with 42K for Reactive-PBT. Preparing the replay corpus takes about 30 minutes plus 1–2 minutes for storage, but it is reused across training seeds. Amortized over four seeds and the full 2 × 109-step budget per seed, the preparation overhead is under 3%. Effective throughput remains about 123K SPS, or 2.9× Reactive-PBT.
